# Charter your first project

In this tutorial we open a new workspace, say what an organisation is
for, start one project, and read the charter Cartograph writes from it.

We use a made-up organisation so you can type exactly what we type: a
produce cooperative. Its members deliver crates of produce to depots,
and retail buyers keep finding faults that the depot should have caught.
It is the same cooperative that ships in Cartograph's example workspace.

You need an empty Cartograph workspace, and you need to be allowed to
edit it. To start one on your own computer, run `cartograph serve
./my-vault` on a new, empty folder and open <http://localhost:8080>
(see [Deploying Cartograph](../../DEPLOYMENT.md)). You do not need to
know any project management words first. Each question tells you what
its word means.

## Part 1: Say what the organisation is for

1. Open Cartograph in your browser. You see the question *Give your
   organisation a name*, with one box and a short definition under it.

2. Type `Example Cooperative` and press **Next**.

   You see *What is the vision of Example Cooperative?*

3. Type `Every member family earns a fair and dependable living from what
   it grows.` and press **Next**.

   You see *What is the mission of Example Cooperative?*

4. Type `We collect, check and sell our members' produce together, so it
   reaches buyers sound and members are paid on time for every crate.`
   and press **Next**.

   You see *Here is Example Cooperative so far*, with the organisation,
   the vision and the mission you wrote.

5. Press **Next**.

   You see *How do you plan to achieve this?* Your vision and mission are
   read back above the box.

6. Type `Raise produce quality` in the first box and press **Next**.

   You see *What does "Raise produce quality" set out to do?*

7. Type `More deliveries meet the quality standard, since catching
   problems early beats handling returns later.` and press **Next**.

   You see *Which objectives sit under "Raise produce quality"?*

8. Type `Hold every depot to one standard` in the first box and press
   **Next**.

   You see *What will be true once "Hold every depot to one standard" is
   met?*

9. Type `Faults are found before produce leaves the depot` in the first
   box and press **Next**.

   You see *The map of Example Cooperative*. It shows the vision, the
   mission, the goal, the objective and the outcome you just wrote.

10. Press **Enter Cartograph**.

    You are on Home, under the question *What are you working on?*

We now have the start of a strategy. The project we start next points at
the outcome we wrote.

## Part 2: Start the project

11. In the box on Home, type `Check every delivery against the quality
    standard at the depot's intake`. Press Enter.

    You see *Define it as*, with a row of kinds: Purpose, Goal,
    Objective, Outcome, Indicator, Gap, Assumption, Portfolio, Programme,
    Operation and Project.

12. Choose **Project**.

    The project walk opens with one question on the screen: *What is your
    project about?* Your words are already in the box.

13. Press **Next**.

    You see *What gaps does it address?*

14. In the box *Name a new gap*, type `Faults are found after dispatch`
    and press **Add**. Then press **Next**.

    You see *Which outcomes does it serve?*

15. Choose *Faults are found before produce leaves the depot*, the
    outcome we wrote in Part 1. Press **Next**.

    You see *Who is it for?*

16. In the box *Name a new group*, type `Delivering members` and press
    **Add**. Then press **Next**.

    You see *Which team owns it?*

17. In the box *Name a new team*, type `Quality Team` and press **Add**.
    Then press **Next**.

    You see *You named new items for this project. Let's fill them in.*
    It starts with the gap.

18. Under *Describe how things are now*, type `One fault in five is found
    at intake; buyers find the rest.` Under *Describe how things should
    be*, type `Most faults are found at intake, before produce leaves the
    depot.` Press **Next**.

    The next item is the group, *Delivering members*.

19. Under *Describe who is in this group*, type `Members who deliver
    produce to a depot during the season.` Press **Done**.

    You see *Ready to start*.

20. In the box *Its name*, type `Quality Check Rollout`. Press **Start the
    project**.

    Naming the project is what creates it. You land on its first stage,
    *Context*, with the project's stages shown on the page: Context, Problem,
    Objectives, Governance, Scope and Deliverables, Plan, Success and
    handover, and Approval. The top bar shows your change set, named
    *Changes to Purpose*, with a count of its changes, and a **Merge**
    button.

## Part 3: Fill in two stages

21. Scroll to the foot of the page and press **Next: problem**.

    The heading reads *Problem*. The page has two sections, *Beneficiaries*
    with *Delivering members* chosen, and *Problem*.

22. Scroll down to *Problems*. A card for the first problem is open.
    Under *This problem is defined as*, type `Four in five quality faults
    are found by a buyer rather than at intake.` Under *The project will
    change this to*, type `Faults are found at the depot's intake, where
    they can still be put right.`

    The map beside the page now shows your problem sentence under
    *PROBLEMS*.

23. At the foot of the page, press **Next: objectives**, then **Next:
    governance**, then **Next: scope and deliverables**.

    The heading reads *Scope and Deliverables*. Scroll down to *In scope*
    and *Out of scope*, side by side.

24. Under *In scope*, press **Add** and type `Intake checks at every
    depot`. Under *Out of scope*, press **Add** and type `Product
    labelling and certification, owned elsewhere`.

    Each sentence appears as a numbered row. We leave the other stages for
    later.

## Part 4: Merge it

25. Press **Merge** in the top bar.

    A box asks *Merge 8 records?* and says *Some items are unfinished.*
    It lists the open items for each record, each one a link to where it
    is finished.

26. In the box *Why merge before these are finished?*, type `First pass;
    the rest follows.` Press **Merge anyway**.

    You see *Merged into the workspace.* Everything we wrote is now part
    of the workspace.

## Part 5: Read the charter

27. Press **Charter**, beside the stage title near the top right of the
    page.

    The charter opens under the heading *Charter*, with **Download PDF**
    and **Open in a tab** above it.

28. Read it. *At a glance* gives what the project is about, its lead
    team, *Quality Team*, and the outcome it serves. *Definition
    readiness* lists what is still open. Further down, *Problem
    statement* holds the problem and the intended change we wrote, and
    *Scope* holds our two sentences. Parts we have not filled in, such as
    *Governance*, are empty and offer a button to add to them.

    That is your first charter.

## What we did

We named an organisation and wrote its vision, mission, goal, objective
and outcome. We started a project that serves the outcome, wrote its
problem and scope, merged it, and read the charter it produced.
