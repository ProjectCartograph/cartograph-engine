# Start a programme

This guide shows you how to start a programme and write what it needs to be a programme: its aim, the problem it answers, the outcomes and indicators it is judged on, and the people who answer for it. The example is the cooperative's *Depot Resilience Programme*.

If you are not sure the work is a programme and not a project or a portfolio, read [About programmes, portfolios and projects](../explanation/about-programmes-portfolios-and-projects.md) first.

Before you start, the outcomes you want the programme to serve should already be in Strategy, and the team that will lead it should already exist.

## Steps

1. In the rail, choose **Programmes**. Press **Programme**. The page *New programme* opens.
2. Type the name in **Name**, for example `Depot Resilience Programme`. Choose a team in **Lead team**, for example *Depot Network Team*. Press **Start**.
3. The programme opens on **Aim**, inside a change set named *Changes to Depot Resilience Programme*. In **Intended change**, say how things will be once the programme has worked, for example `Every depot stays open through the whole season`. In **Benefit**, say who is better off and how, for example `Members can deliver on any day of the season`. If the aim comes from a published plan, put where in **Source**. Press **Next**.
4. On **Problems**, press **Problem**. Fill in **This problem is defined as**, for example `Some depots close for repairs without warning, so members arrive with produce and cannot deliver`. In **It affects**, choose the groups the problem lands on, for example *Delivering members*. In **It is related to identified gaps**, choose the gap that shows it, if one is recorded. In **The project will change this to**, say what will be true once it is solved, for example `Depots plan every closure and members are told ahead`. Press **Next**.
5. On **Alignment**, choose the outcomes the programme serves under **Outcomes**, and the indicators it is judged by under **Indicators**. Under each heading, Cartograph offers matches under *Suggested from what you wrote*. You can pick one of those, or choose from the lists below. For example, choose the outcome *Depot downtime is planned, not discovered* and the indicator *Depot availability*. Press **Next**.
6. **Theory of change** is where you write how the projects lead to the outcome. It takes its own guide: [Write a programme's theory of change](write-a-programmes-theory-of-change.md). Press **Next** to carry on, or do it now.
7. Press **Next** to leave **Risks** for later.
8. On **Components**, tick each project or programme this programme depends on, for example *Quality Check Rollout*. Then give a reason in the box that opens, under *What this needs from it*. If the projects are not written yet, leave this step and come back.
9. Press **Next** past **Stakeholders**. On **Governance**, choose the **Sponsor**, the **Programme manager** and the **Business change manager**. Each is a role, not a person. The **Lead team** is already set.
10. Press **Merge** at the top of the page. If some items are unfinished, the dialog *Merge 1 record?* lists them and asks *Why merge before these are finished?* Give a reason and press **Merge anyway**, or press **Cancel** and finish them first.

At any step, the **Checks** panel under the form says what is missing. It advises and never stops you saving. To read the programme as a document, press **Charter** at the top of the page.
